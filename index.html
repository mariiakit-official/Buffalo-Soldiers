03 · PREPARE FOR WAR

The battle of perfection.

A realistic interpersonal challenge

Our i.c.stars team is preparing for a project demonstration, and I want everything to be perfect. Deep down, I am afraid that any mistake will reflect on me and show that I am not good enough. So I take on most of the work myself, double-check everything, and struggle to hand tasks to my teammates. I am tired and stretched thin, but I cannot let go. The real battle is not with my team. It is with my need to be perfect, and it is affecting how I work with them.

Who is involved: me and my fear of imperfection, my project teammates, who could carry more if I made space for them, and the demo deadline that makes every detail feel critical.

THE INTERNAL CONFLICT

Precision leads the charge: “It has to be flawless, so I should do it myself.” Resilience says, “Keep pushing — you can handle it,” and Ambition wants the demo to be impressive. On the other side, Leadership and Courage answer: “Good enough and shared is better than perfect and alone.” Choice is caught in the middle, worried that letting go means the work, and I, will be judged as not good enough.

How I choose to lead my army

  1. Pause and notice the overload · Choice

    Notice when perfectionism is driving me, and separate real risks from my fear of being seen as not good enough.

  2. Share the work by strengths · Leadership

    Divide the remaining tasks based on each teammate’s skills and interests, so everyone owns a part of the demo.

  3. Define “good enough” · Precision

    Agree together on what “done” looks like, focus my checking on the highest risks, and stop redoing other people’s work.

  4. Ask for help openly · Courage

    “I’ve been trying to make everything perfect and took on too much. Can we rebalance the tasks so we all own a part of the demo?”

  5. Protect my energy · Resilience + Devotion

    Rest and keep time for my family, so I can show up as my best self for the team and at home.

THE BEST POSSIBLE OUTCOME

The work is shared fairly, my teammates take ownership, and we deliver a strong demo together. It may not be perfect, but it is excellent, and I no longer carry everything alone or burn out trying to be flawless.

LEADERSHIP LESSON

My need to be perfect was really a fear of not being good enough. Leadership means accepting that shared work can be imperfect and still be excellent, and trusting others to contribute.

04 · MY REFLECTION

Becoming the commander
of my own army.

My journey from law and entrepreneurship into technology has taught me that growth is rarely comfortable. Courage helps me start. Resilience helps me continue. Devotion reminds me what matters most.

Precision wants to get the details right, while Curiosity wants to try something new. Ambition looks toward the future, while Choice reminds me to pause and think. Leadership is learning to listen to all of them and move forward with intention.

I want to become a confident technology professional, a thoughtful leader, and a compassionate human being. Success isn't only about a title or an achievement; it is also about how I treat others and the example I set for my daughter.

I am still becoming. And that is my strength.
